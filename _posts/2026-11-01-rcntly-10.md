I enjoy reading monthly updates from [people][arne] [I][macwright]
[follow][duarte] (via RSS), they're always so interesting. So I figured I'd try
writing my own. It'll be good to 1) get me into a regular writing cadence (I
want to write more, here and elsewhere, this year), and 2) maybe someone will
find mine interesting (jk I don't think people read this blog). Anyways, let's
give this a try!

## general

As hinted at in my last [rcntly][rcntly09], we spent a week in Prague at the
beginning of October. It was a lovely trip, Prague is a devastatingly beautiful
city. I've plans to write a recap of the trip (with more words and pictures),
but it's not yet ready at time of publication.

Now that the weather is cooling off, I'm getting back into running. The summer
is better for biking, I can handle the heat a lot better on my bike. Now that
the weather is cooling and the winds are picking up and biting, biking becomes
less of a desirable activity; and inversely running becomes more of a desirable
activity. It's not about bundling, but for me running is _harder_, so I build
up more body heat, and with different routes and slower pace I handle the
cooler temperatures much better. And running is fun! I've been sticking to 5ks
down Navy Pier for now as I build up my running legs again, and then who knows
- maybe I can extend and do some longer runs.

## using

On the running front, I retired my HOKA Skyflows and replaced them with a pair
of Adidas ADIZERO EVO SLs (that's a mouthful). They are easily the lightest and
fastest running shoes I've ever owned, but are still classed as daily trainers.
I'm 4 runs in and really enjoying them. I'm still hella slow, but the lightness
and bounciness is much appreciated[^1].

Sadly, I left my ereader (Kobo) in the seat back pocket[^2] on our final flight
of the Prague trip (CPH --> ORD). So when we got back I dusted off my old
Kindle (from 2015 or 2017??), jailbroke it like a good hacker and set it free.
As far as I can tell to read Libby books from the library I still need to use
the built in reading app, but I do have [KOReader][] installed and still need
to configure that. I do think I will get another Kobo Libra Colour at some
point, because I really like the physical buttons to change pages. Also, while
ereader technology hasn't changed _that_ much in 10 years, the screen refresh
rates have improved and that makes a _massive_ difference in terms of quality
of life.

I did go scorched earth on my nvim config like I threatened last month. I
rebuilt it from scratch, using the new native nvim package manager. 225 lines
of config, 15 plugins, single file. So far so good. I also updated my old vim
config a bit, and have been using that on and off as well. I think overall nvim
might be the better choice for longevity and plugin options (I don't want too
many, but some are quite handy), but I'm enjoying plain vim. Either way, I'm
going for a smaller, simpler config that's snappy and gets out of me way. Modal
editing is a given.

## reading

I had a lot of books going this month, and am finishing them all at right about
the same time, coincidentally at the end of the month. _Theo of Golden_ for
book club (it was... alright), _Orbital_ by Samantha Harvey (beautiful writing,
not much happened), and _The Man Who Died Twice_ by Richard Osman (The Os-man
is a delight). I feel I have a much less focused reading plan this year
compared to last. Which has been kind of nice, but I also feel a bit adrift and
have ended up reading some books that I picked off my list at random that I
didn't enjoy as much in the moment as I thought I might. Live and learn, and
maybe that's something to take into account for next year.

## watching

_Ludwig_ Series 2 is out, and we're half way through it. David Mitchell is a
treasure as usual, but I feel the overall series arc isn't as good as the
first. Still really enjoying it, though.

We finished Series 3 of _The Marlow Murder Club_ and the _Marble Hall Murders_.
Both good, cosy, murder-y.

It will soon be time to start watching Christmas-y movies again. I'll have to
resurrect our list of favorite films and start working our way through them.

## listening

Deafheaven released their EP _Working in the Fields of the Bomb_ at the
beginning of the month, and it is by far the heaviest thing they've done to
date. Full on black metal. Heavy. It good.
