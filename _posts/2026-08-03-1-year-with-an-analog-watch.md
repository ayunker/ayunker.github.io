Last year I ditched my Apple Watch and started wearing an analog watch. I'm a
huge fan, and here are some thoughts after a year. None of this is
groundbreaking (and much of it has probably been said before), but I wanted to
put it in my own words.

Pickle got me a [Vaer S3][s3] last year for Father's Day. It's a 36mm
quartz-powered field watch. I looked at watches for a _long_ time, and this is
the one that always stood out to me. Pickle has excellent taste.

It's a field watch, so it's a little utilitarian but highly useful (a tool
watch, if you will). Compact size, great for me small wrists, highly legible
dial with great lume. Very durable. With it's metal bracelet, it reminds me a
bit of a Rolex Explorer, but I usually wear it with a nylon strap for comfort
and simplicity.

I had my Apple Watch for about a year, but I wasn't in love with it. I got it
for two reasons: **1** to lessen my reliance on my phone (and not have it with
me all the time), and **2** to track my running and biking without needing to
carry my phone with me.

But after a year, I still had my phone with me all the time. If I was sitting
on the couch and I had a notification on my watch, I'd need to get up and grab
my phone to action it. I wasn't using my phone any less. The Apple Watch was ok
for workouts, but my watch was less accurate than Kat's watch. If we ran a 5K
together, I could be up to half a kilometer off from her measurements, despite
us running side by side. It didn't feel super accurate.

Plus, I didn't like that I needed to charge it every day or so. It wasn't
incredibly inconvenient, but it was another thing that needed my attention,
another cable to bring when traveling. The juice was not worth the squeeze.

So I upgraded, and it's been great! The Vaer tells time excellently. It does
have a battery in it, but it should last a good 3-5 years. It does not pester
me with notifications. Plus, did I mention how stunning it looks?

The Apple Watch was not the forcing function I thought it would be to reduce my
phone usage. I don't know if the Vaer helps that any more or less. I think
phone usage is more of a discipline/systems problem than a technological one.
Though if I just need to check the time, an analog watch saves me a phone
pickup just as much, if not more, than an Apple Watch[^pickup].

The transition to the new watch was mostly seamless. I did notice I felt ghost
pings for a few days. I would *feel* a buzz from a notification, only to
realize that I no longer receive notifications on my wrist. The psychological
implications of that was a little spooky[^craig].

I really don't miss the Apple Watch at all, but there is one use case where it
did come in handy. The Apple Watch could give you real-time directions as you
walked, including a buzz as you approached the intersection where you needed to
turn as a heads up. That was genuinely useful[^genuine] in a foreign city like
London, where phone theft on the street is a concern.

I don't really have a solution for that with an analog watch. It's a bit less
convenient, but I guess I just need to study the route a bit more ahead of time
and have a good grip on the phone when I do pull it out on the street. We've
navigated for centuries without this convenience, it's not the end of the
world[^map].

There is one more thing to note. Hear me out, I don't think this negates any of
the reasoning in this post:

I did get a Coros watch for running/cycling tracking. It *is* a smart watch,
but very much focused towards fitness. Technically you can get notifications
and wear it daily as a smart watch/Apple Watch replacement. But I don't. It
only gets used when I go for a run or bike ride. It's easier to bring with than
a phone, plus its GPS is more accurate. I don't consider this to take anything
away from the switch though. The Coros isn't a daily watch. It's a tool with a
very specific, narrow use case. I would never wear it out and about or even
around the house.

So that's it. I switched from an Apple Watch to an analog watch. I'm chuffed as
chips. If you're thinking of making the switch: go for it, give it a shot! You
might like it too.
