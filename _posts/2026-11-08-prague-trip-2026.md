I think we're going to do this trip recap a little bit differently. For our
past London trips I wrote a travelogue, but I don't think that will be as
interesting for Prague. This trip had less of an itinerary and was more of a
sightseeing exploration. So let's try breaking it down by categories: food,
coffee/matcha, getting around, sightseeing, and miscellaneous.

## food

Overall the food was good in Prague. As vegetarians (well, I'm not 100%
vegetarian but close enough for consideration when looking at food options),
Prague's meat and cheese heavy cuisine was a bit challenging. However, there
were plenty of vegan/vegetarian restaurants to choose from, both serving Czech
and other cuisines. We had lovely vegan takes on traditional dishes at [Vegan's
Prague][vegan_prague].

included an appetizer or beer/lemonade. Which I guess is pretty good.

## getting around

Prague is a small city, and quite easily walkable. For traveling longer
distances, the tram and metro are super convenient.

It's tough to judge the scale of a city from a map. A map of London, to me,
looks like everything is within walking distance. But you wouldn't want to walk
from Big Ben to the Tower of London unless you had a lot of time on your hands.
Prague is the opposite. Getting from the New Town to the Old Town looks like a
hike, but in reality is only like a 15 minute walk, tops. Many of the streets
are pedestrian only, or pedestrian forward. There isn't a ton of traffic flow
control (like stoplights or signs). But there's strength in numbers, and the
ratio of pedestrians to cars is high enough that it feels very safe to walk.

However, Prague is also a hilly city. The center of town (Old Town, New Town)
are flat, but once you get out a bit, the hills begin. And some of those hills
are _hills_. Getting up to Prague Castle felt almost like climbing a mountain,
and some of the roads had switchbacks to get up there. The hills provided some
breathtaking views, but left you breathless on the way up, too.

{%
  include fig.html
  src="/assets/images/prague2026/hills.jpg"
%}

The public transit is quite lovely when you need it. There's an extensive tram
system that runs along the road network, and many popular lines run every 3-5
minutes. Others run every 10-15 minutes, but that's still pretty good. The
metro is only 3 lines, and I think is used more for longer distances - getting
from one end of the city to the other. Especially if you're visiting, I think
the tram system can cover the majority of getting around.

The payment system is interesting. I like it, but in many places I just don't
think it would work. You buy a time-based ticket, I think starting from half an
hour up to 72 hours[^2]. But it's kind of on the honor system - you don't scan
on/off either the tram or metro. This is what makes it some time efficient,
with the number of people hopping on or off at any given stop, you could get
stuck for minutes processing all the people. But! There are ticket wardens that
do the rounds and hop on a tram and ensure everyone has a ticket. It happened
to us a couple of times, and I'm not sure what happens if you don't have a
ticket (kicked off? fined? publicly shamed? all of the above?).

So it's a wonderfully efficient system, but I think you need a certain
community/civic dedication to make it work. The checks are few and far between,
so you need a society that will reliably adhere to the system. More community
oriented societies, like that in Europe or Asia[^3] could employ this with
success. In the US (or even England/London) there's no chance of it succeeding.
Too many people would not buy tickets, try to "beat the system", get their own
or whatever. But in Prague, it worked a treat.

## sightseeing

Prague Castle is the largest castle in the world, depending on how you define
castle. That's not a dig or anything, but it's not a castle that's like one big
building. Prague Castle is a big area fortified by stone walls, but it has 2
cathedrals, other government buildings and multiple squares. The president of
Czechia even lives and works there now.

## miscellaneous

One of the unexpected highlights of the trip was feeding the nutria, otherwise
known as river rats. They kinda look like capybaras, and they live on islands
on the Vitava river. They are partial to carrots, and will walk up and grab
them nicely from you if offered.
